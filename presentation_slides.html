Problem Statement

Why This Project Was Needed

The Farming Problem:

Farmers in India suffer crop losses due to erratic rainfall and wrong chemical dosages. They need to know their expected yield before harvest so they can plan loans, storage, and prices.

The Major Machine Learning Mistake (Target Leakage):

Many student projects use Production to predict Yield. But mathematically:

Yield = Production / Area

If you already know total production, you don't need machine learning! That is called Target Leakage. We completely removed 'Production' so the model predicts yield before harvest!

CO1: Data Preparation

Dataset & Feature Engineering

Dataset Overview:

  • Indian Ministry of Agriculture official dataset (1997–2020)
  • 19,689 total agricultural records across 55 crops and 30 states
  • Features: Crop, State, Season, Area, Annual Rainfall, Fertilizer, Pesticide, Crop Year

Why We Engineered 'Fertilizer per Area':

A small 2-hectare family farm and a 500-hectare commercial farm use very different total amounts of fertilizer. We calculated Fertilizer / Area (kg/ha) to compare all farms fairly on an equal scale.

CO1: Operational Alert

The Underperformance Risk Alert

What is the Underperformance Flag?

It is an automatic early-warning flag (1 or 0) that triggers when:

  • Rainfall is ample (> 800 mm)
  • Fertilizer is properly applied (> 20 kg/ha)
  • BUT realized yield collapses to less than 50% of normal for that crop

Why is it used in real life?

When weather and fertilizer are good but yield is still low, it alerts farmers to hidden pest attacks, disease, or bad seed quality early before complete financial ruin occurs.

CO1: Exploratory Analysis

Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA)

What We Analyzed:

  • Missing Values: Verified 0 missing values after cleaning.
  • Correlation Heatmap: Analyzed non-linear relationships between weather inputs and crop yields.
  • Outlier Audits: Used Interquartile Range (IQR) boxplots to inspect farm sizes and fertilizer dosages.

Why Outlier Audits Are Important:

Agricultural data is heavily right-skewed. Using log transformations and IQR checks prevents giant commercial farms from distorting the models for smaller farmers.

CO2: Supervised Learning

Linear Models & Regularization

Models Compared:

  • Linear Regression (OLS): Basic straight-line mathematical baseline.
  • Ridge Regression (L2): Shrinks weights smoothly to prevent features from interfering with each other.
  • Lasso Regression (L1): Sets useless feature weights to exact zero (automatic feature selection).
  • ElasticNet: Balanced combination of both L1 and L2 penalties.

Why Regularization is Used:

It solves the Bias-Variance trade-off. Unregularized models easily overfit noisy weather records, while Ridge and Lasso produce stable, reliable predictions.

CO3: Ensemble Learning

Tree-Based Ensemble Models

Bagging vs. Boosting Architectures:

  • Decision Tree: Single tree (fast, but high variance/overfitting).
  • Random Forest (Bagging): Trains many independent trees in parallel to reduce variance.
  • AdaBoost & XGBoost (Boosting): Trains sequential shallow trees where each tree fixes errors from the previous tree.

Why Tree Ensembles Outperform Linear Models:

Farming biology is non-linear. Adding more fertilizer helps only up to a point, after which it burns the crop. Tree ensembles capture these biological limits much better than straight lines.

CO4: Unsupervised Learning

Clustering & Dimensionality Reduction

Unsupervised Techniques Used (No Labels):

  • PCA: Scree plot shows the first 2 principal components capture dominant weather and farm scale variance.
  • K-Means Clustering: Elbow method identified optimal k = 4 clusters.
  • DBSCAN: Density-based clustering automatically detected abnormal recording errors and extreme climate noise points.

Why It is Used:

It proves the dataset naturally separates into 4 distinct farming styles in India: (1) Arid subsistence, (2) Irrigated cash crops, (3) Commercial plantations, and (4) Monsoon rainfed staples.

CO5: Evaluation & Validation

Model Evaluation & Validation

Our Academic Evaluation Protocol:

  • 3-Way Split: 70% Train, 15% Validation, 15% Holdout Test to prevent hyperparameter leakage.
  • 5-Fold Cross Validation: Proves consistent high stability across all data slices with average R² = 0.988.
  • ROC-AUC (0.764) & PR-AUC (0.447): Measures risk classification on imbalanced rare failure events.
  • Brier Score (0.091): Reliability diagram verifies predicted probability percentages match true empirical frequencies.

Why We Benchmark Hyperparameter Search:

Bayesian Optimization reached the highest validation score in only 30 iterations and 4.6 seconds—which is 4 times faster than exhaustive Grid Search!
